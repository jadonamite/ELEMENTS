# ELEMENTS: lore and structure

The source of truth for the world, built one aspect at a time. Each aspect is broken down by plot, mode, story, campaign and battle. Where this file and `GAME-DESIGN.md` disagree, this file wins.

## 1. The premise

### Plot
1. **Sign up.** The player chooses male or female.
2. **Make the character.** Simple choices: race, skin colour and presets.
3. **The physical world.** A short film: the player's own character, in our ordinary world, buys the game ELEMENTS and takes it home to play.
4. **The glitch.** They start the game at home, it glitches, and they're dragged into the world of ELEMENTS.
5. **The rising.** Inside ELEMENTS the character hovers up in a level-up state. This is where they become Dim or Lit, and if Lit, which nation.
6. **The goal.** They're trapped inside the game and want to get out. Only the Allborn can help them, so they set out to find the Allborn.
7. **The twist they don't know yet.** The Allborn is a myth. Nobody in the world knows that for certain; there are only rumours.

### The rising decides, not the player
The world decides during the rising; the player never chooses. It is the same lottery as before: 85% of human players come down Lit, 15% come down Dim. If Lit, the world also decides the nation. Vaknas (Dim who later awaken) can be at most 0.5% of the total population.

### Mode
- Sign-up and character creation are menus.
- The physical world and the glitch are a short film, not playable.
- The rising is a staged moment the player watches, ending in their result.

### Story

You pay for ELEMENTS at a shop counter and carry it home. You set the case beside the console, sit down and press play.

The picture catches. You press again. A split runs across the screen and keeps going, through the room. You reach for the edge of the table. Your fingers slide off. You are dragged through before you can get a grip.

Inside ELEMENTS, you rise. Your feet hang above the ground; your hands reach for something to hold. You recognise the pause before a game announces a level gained. This time you are the body waiting for the result.

You have no choice in it. You come down Lit, with an element and a nation, or Dim, without an element. Either way, you are still here. You want your room back. You want to put the controller down and leave.

You ask how to get home. You hear a name: the Allborn. Supposedly, they command all four elements. Supposedly, they alone can get you out. You have only rumours to go on.

You ask where to find them. You follow the first lead.

### Film script

Use the exact character the player creates, retaining their face, skin colour, hair and body choices throughout. Fit ordinary clothing to that character for the physical-world shots; keep it through the rising. No voice-over or spoken exposition.

Build three compact sets: a shop counter, a short night street with a doorway, and a small room. Dress them with free scanned surfaces and props; plain box meshes with those surfaces suffice for the unbranded screen, console and game case. The case needs only the ELEMENTS title. Reuse a small patch of the game's stone floor for the rising, with the surroundings out of frame. Lighting and camera placement carry the scale; there is no crowd or large city set.

Open: Where does the rising happen? The floor patch is provisional staging, with no landmark, insignia or ceremony establishing a location.

Open: What causes the glitch and drags the character in, if that is ever explained? Show the physical pull without an identifiable agent, voice or symbol.

### Campaign
The search for the Allborn is the spine of the whole game. Every chapter is a lead: a rumour, a relic, a witness, a sealed place. The truth (the Allborn is a myth) is held back until late, and players are never told it outright early on.

### Battle
Not part of this aspect. The first fight comes after the rising, in the player's new home.

### The film's look
In-engine, using the game's own characters, materials and lighting, like the Shadow Fight 3 cinematic trailer: real camera work and cuts, not a different art style.

## 3. The nations and the power over them

Agreed 29 Oct. The plot here will be refined later.

### Plot
- **The seat of the Edict rules.** It now holds the economic power and the army, and can oppress the nations at will. The one who held it before was far more amenable and benign, and established the peace. The one who holds it now is power-hungry, afraid of the nations and afraid of a coup.
- **The nations** bow to that seat in public and resent it in private. Each has its own ruler.
- **The nations fight each other.** There is a lot of world play between them, to be enlarged with later builds.

### The nations

| Nation | Colour | Land |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IGNIS | Crimson | A volcanic forge city: black stone, ember-lit streets |
| AQUES | Azure | A river delta: stilt houses and canals |
| TERRA | Ochre | A canyon fortress cut into ochre cliffs |
| AURA | Pale white | High cliffs: wind towers and rope bridges |

### Character of each nation (30 Oct)
- **IGNIS:** at war with everyone. Arrogant, ambitious, the most influential nation.
- **TERRA:** the richest nation after Chromara.
- **AQUES:** the healers. Where people go to recuperate and be healed.
- **AURA:** peaceable toward everybody. There, but not there.

### Wars between nations
The nations are at war from the start, as a state of the world: hostile borders, wary NPCs, prices and routes that shift. There are no collective battles that players join.

### Mode
- **The beginning story mode happens in your home nation.** You train, fight, take your first leads, and make your first financial decisions there.
- **The word is the town.** Everyone talks about going to town (Chromara), and the player wants to go, but they can choose not to.

### Story
- **Newcomers are younglings.** A new player is treated as someone who has just come of age and started to see their potential, whatever their actual age.
- **The Dim are lowborn.** Some people value them as crafters, but they are still lowborn.
- **NPCs have their own characters.** Some are kind, others don't care at all.

### Campaign
Each nation holds one piece of the search for the Allborn: four chapters, one per nation, each ending with that nation's master giving up their rumour.

### Battle
- **The enemies are villains, not husks:** robbers, soldiers, miscreants, level bosses and monsters. "Husk" is retired as an idea.
- Nations at war with each other is part of the world, built out later.

## 4. Chromara and the seat of the Edict

Agreed 29 Oct.

### Plot
- **The Edict** is both the law that banished the old names of the elements and the seat of power that issues it. Whoever holds the seat is the Lord.
- **The benign Lord before** vanished, and the current Lord took the seat in the confusion. Rumours tie the vanishing to the Allborn.
- **Why the names were banished:** the current Lord fears the nations, and names carry power. Unnamed elements are weaker and harder to teach, which keeps the nations in check.

### Mode
- **The map:** Chromara takes about 40% of the world; each of the four nations about 15%.
- **Chromara** is the big city at the centre: the main market, the treasury, the Sentries, guild halls and the main story leads, and the place most players meet. Some things only happen in Chromara, but not everything does.
- **Every nation has markets too,** and most boss battles happen out in the nations and other locations, not in Chromara.

### Story
- **The Lord's force:** the Sentries keep order in the city, and the army garrisons border forts in every nation. The soldiers among the villains are the Lord's soldiers.

### Campaign
Exposing or overthrowing the Lord is the second thread of the story, beside the search for the Allborn. The two meet near the end.

### Battle
- **Chromara is chaotic,** and how chaotic depends on the player's decisions.
- **Trouble has a price.** At worst, a player who causes trouble is taken to jail, loses everything they have, and is thrown out with nothing.

## 5. Villains, monsters, bosses and showdowns

Agreed 29 Oct. Monsters and bosses will be broadened later; they need more imagination to carry the gameplay and the story.

### Who you fight
- **Robbers and miscreants:** street gangs in Chromara's back streets, and deserters from the Lord's army on the roads between nations.
- **Soldiers:** the Lord's army.
- **Monsters:** beasts of the wild lands between nations, each shaped by its element (for example ember hounds near IGNIS, river serpents near AQUES). To be broadened.
- **Level bosses:** one at the end of each nation's chapter, guarding that nation's master's rumour (for example a rogue IGNIS warlord holding a forge town). To be broadened.
- **Husks:** kept as an idea for world-building later; not the main enemy.

### Monsters and bosses (agreed 30 Oct)
- **Where monsters come from:** elemental power with no name left to hold it, taking the shape of local beasts. The wild lands, where nobody speaks the names at all, have the most.
- **How they fight:** like animals and forces, not people. They charge, swarm, burrow and fly, have weak spots instead of blocks, and some can only be hurt by the element that counters them.
- **Many kinds, met often:** more than a handful per nation's wild lands, plus a rare one per nation worth hunting (its body yields a rare material). Monsters turn up often, in line with the rule that showdowns come as often as they can without becoming a chore (section 8).
- **Nation bosses are people:** someone who chose power over their own people. Monsters get their own bosses in the dungeons.
- **The Lord is unseen,** felt only through the Sentries and the Edict.

### Bending among NPCs
Most NPCs cannot bend: NPCs are 20% Lit and 80% Dim (Jadon, 1 Nov). There are no Vakna among NPCs. Robbers are mostly Dim and fight with weapons; some soldiers and every boss are Lit.

### Showdowns
- **Any showdown can happen anywhere.** Fights don't need a special place.
- **Preset fight arenas** are built to fit each wider location (a market, a canyon road, a canal quay) and recycled wherever that kind of place appears.
- **Player against player:** another player can challenge you to a brawl. If you accept, the two of you fight it out in a showdown arena, whatever your bending style.

### Jail
Replaced 30 Oct by section 10: jail seizes 40% of the player's CHROMA, materials and gear, vault included, plus a fine to get out. Land is never seized.

## 6. Bending, weapons, hands and legs

Agreed 29 Oct.

### Loot
- Villains mostly drop the CHROMA they were carrying.
- Whoever wins a fight takes the loser's loot: CHROMA, carried items and shards.
- **Physical drop collection (1 Nov):** Loot drops into the physical world at the point of defeat. The player must walk over to claim it; items are never vacuumed automatically into satchels.

### Bending is who you are
- A Lit player can bend at any time; it never runs out. Bending is not fuelled by a meter.
- **Combos use a meter.** Landing hits fills it, and bending combos spend it.
- **The player chooses whether to bend.** Some zones restrict bending, and most people can't tell a Lit from a Dim, so a Lit player can fight without bending and go undercover.
- **The player switches freely** between bending and not bending, and between fighting with the weapon (whatever it is), the hands and the legs.

### The Dim
With enough shards, a Dim can use a very small amount of elemental bending.

### What each element does
| Element | Needs a source? | In a fight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IGNIS | No, bends anywhere | Fire that is thrown, spreads and keeps burning |
| AURA | No, bends anywhere | Wind: dashes, blades of air, pushing enemies away |
| AQUES | Yes: can't bend where there is no water | Water whips, pools that slow, a little healing, and ice combos |
| TERRA | Yes: can't bend where there is no earth | Rocks torn from the ground and shot at the opponent, walls raised for protection |

### Counters (30 Oct)
Elements have only a slight edge over each other, and not a fixed always-on cycle: no "water always beats fire" rule. The edge depends on the situation (terrain, weather, the move used), and skill always decides the fight.

### Weapons
Each nation favours a weapon (IGNIS the sword, AQUES the spear or glaive, TERRA the hammer or axe, AURA the staff, Japanese-style war fans or an air glider), but the player's choice and the materials they craft from decide what they actually carry.

**Weapons while bending (30 Oct):**
- IGNIS keeps any weapon in hand while bending; the blade carries the fire.
- AQUES and TERRA can't hold a weapon while bending. Turning bending on puts the weapon away and they bend bare-handed; drawing the weapon turns bending off.
- AURA bends with a weapon only if it is a staff, war fans or a glider. Any other weapon goes away while bending, as for AQUES and TERRA.

### Hands and legs
Bare-handed fighting uses fists and kicks as equal styles: faster and closer than a weapon, shorter reach. A Lit player bends more strongly bare-handed than with a weapon in hand.

### Ultimates
One signature move per element, opened at tier 5 (section 7).

### Left out for now
Recovering the element's banished true name through mastery.

## 7. Growth

Agreed 29 Oct.
- **Permanent growth has 7 tiers.** Each tier is harder to reach than the one before.
- **Death out in the world:** you wake at your nation's shrine and drop the materials you were carrying.

### What the tiers open (agreed 30 Oct)
- Nobody learns new moves by levelling. The moves exist from the start; tiers decide what you are allowed to use.
- Access opens in this order, a step with each tier: stronger attacks, then better weapons, then combos, then the ultimate at tier 5.
- Tiers 6 and 7 make bending far stronger.
- Each tier takes fighting enough to qualify, paying the master, and winning the master's trial (section 11).
- Tiers are permanent; nobody drops a tier.
- Character levels with experience may come later, once there are more features. Not now.
- **Tier names (fighters):** Tiro, Ictus, Armiger, Nexus, Signum, Profundus, Primus.

### The Dim
- 7 crafting tiers, opened the same way: craft enough, pay a master smith, pass a test piece.
- **Tier names (the Dim):** Apprentice, Journeyman, Artisan, Markman, Master, Assayer, Alderman.
- Awakening to Vakna: tried only from crafting tier 4, once a season, at a shrine in a nation the world picks. Each attempt burns CHROMA; the chance shrinks as the population grows.

## 11. How the game plays

Agreed 30 Oct.

### The loop
Walk a town, talk to people and pick up a lead or a job, travel and get ambushed on the way, fight, take the loot, spend it on gear, training or land, then follow the next lead. Between leads the world is open to wander.

### One shared world
- Everyone shares the world everywhere: towns, roads, wild lands and Chromara.
- Dungeon raids and some party fights are private: alone or with your own party.

### Losing
- Dying out in the world: you wake at your nation's shrine and drop the materials you carried (section 7).
- Losing a story or private fight is more severe than a plain retry and less severe than losing all your loot. Proposed: you lose a quarter of the CHROMA and materials you carry, and your gear takes heavy wear. The exact share is tuned in play.

### Fight rules
- **Block and parry.** Holding block soaks most of the damage. Blocking just as a hit lands is a parry: the attacker staggers and is open.
- **No stamina bar.** Dodges and heavy attacks are limited only by how long the moves take.
- **Healing in a fight:** potions and crafted items (drinking leaves you open), and AQUES's own healing. Nothing else.
- **Aim assist on every device.** Attacks turn a little toward the nearest enemy and lock-on picks a target by itself; the player can switch the lock to another enemy. Because every device gets the same help, anyone can duel anyone.
- **Phone layout** follows a common fighting-game pattern: a movement stick on the left, a cluster of round attack buttons on the right, special moves in a small diamond in the top corner. Exact buttons are settled with the phone pass.
- **Parked until it becomes a concern (leaning yes on all three):** separate punch, kick and weapon buttons instead of switching style; a held direction changing the move; the element throw in the main button cluster with uppercut, slam, dash and ultimate in the corner diamond.

### Growth through the tiers
Each of the 7 tiers takes all three steps, in order: fight enough to qualify, pay the master in CHROMA, then win the master's trial fight.

### The Dim
- Crafting is a short forging mini-game; timing decides quality.
- The Dim follow the same campaign, but reach some leads by trade and favours instead of fighting.

### Travel
- You walk a road the first time. Roads are where ambushes happen.
- Fast travel to places you've already been costs a rare, expensive material, so nobody spams it.

### Fights between players
An accepted duel risks only the stake both players agreed on. Full looting happens only in marked lawless zones, which players enter knowing the risk.

### Gear
Gear has stats and rarity, wears down with use, and only the Dim repair it.

### Devices
Phones at launch, alongside mouse and keyboard and game controllers. The laptop comes first; phones are tuned after. Everything built now carries a quality setting so the phone pass is a tune, not a rebuild.

## 12. Land and buildings

Agreed 30 Oct.

### Ownership
- At launch, land and buildings live on the game server only. Once the game is finished and people are playing, land and buildings are issued as a larger NFT collection on NEAR.

### Where land is
- A district of plots at the edge of each nation's town, and a small, very expensive district in Chromara.

### Theft from plots
- Breaking into a plot's stores counts as robbing (a red flag). Players are not told this is possible: they find out.
- Raids are a separate thing, still to be designed.
- While offline, a player can lose at most 10 to 20% of everything they own to robbery.

### Limits
- Up to 3 plots per player. Clans can hold more land together; the exact numbers are found in play.

### Upkeep
- Unpaid buildings decay. After about 30 days unpaid, the plot goes back on sale and the owner gets part of its price back.

### Home
- A house on your plot becomes where you wake, instead of the shrine, and has its own store. Jail's 40% reaches the home store as it reaches the vault.

## 13. Guilds and clans

Agreed 30 Oct.

### Two sizes
- **Guild:** up to 30 members. Founding one costs a great deal of CHROMA.
- **Clan:** up to 50 members. A guild becomes a clan by upgrading, which costs more again.
- The AI rival is a clan of AI players.

### Who joins
Anyone: any nation, Lit and Dim alike. A group with its own Dim crafters is stronger.

### What a group shares
- A hall on its land, a shared vault (the leader decides who can take what), its pooled land limit (section 12), and a banner shown above members' heads.

### Groups against groups
- Wars are declared and accepted by both sides. Fights inside a declared war carry no red flags.
- Groups race each other for the world boss's final blow.

### Leaving
A member who leaves or is thrown out takes nothing from the shared vault; land they paid for stays with the group.

## 14. Dungeon raids

Agreed 30 Oct.

### Where
Ruins from before the Edict, under each nation and in the wild lands, where the old names of the elements are still carved into the walls.

### Who goes in
- Normal dungeons take 1 to 5. Go alone, with other players, or with NPC Lit fighters at your side.
- A few large dungeons take up to 15 and need a guild or clan.

### Inside
Hand-built rooms shuffled into a new order each run, ending in a dungeon boss.

### What they give
- Rare materials, gear and shards, never treasury CHROMA. The expensive material that fast travel costs comes mainly from dungeons.
- A general market run by an NPC buys rare materials, gear and shards for CHROMA, so a party can sell straight after a raid.

### Getting in
Every run needs a key that only the Dim can craft.

### Losing
As with any lost story or private fight: a quarter of what you carry and heavy gear wear (section 11).

### The story inside
Carvings and old records about the Allborn that contradict each other. Each one feeds the hope that kills.

## 15. The living world

Agreed 30 Oct.

### NPC fighters
Hired for CHROMA, paid per run. Better fighters cost more. A hired fighter can die and has to be replaced.

### The world pushes back
When one nation dominates, the world reacts by itself (for example a firestorm that closes a trade route).

### The Sentries
The Lord's enforcers of the Edict (section 4). They patrol Chromara and the roads near it only, so the far roads stay lawless.

### Day and night
Robbers are bolder at night, some monsters come out only after dark, and shops shut.

### NPC memory
Every NPC keeps one score for each player: how they feel about you. Cheating lowers it, favours raise it, and it changes prices and what they say.

### The Raid Hall
Run by the Dim, in Chromara with a branch in each nation. It sells the keys the Dim craft, hires out NPC fighters, posts jobs for parties, and settles complaints (a fighter who ran, a bad loot split, a cheated buyer), keeping a record of who has been reported. Dim players can work there for a cut.

### Nations at war, for one player
A player of a nation at war (IGNIS above all) may enter another nation but is not welcome: higher prices, guards who watch them closely, no attack on sight.

### AQUES heals
Gear wear and lasting injuries from hard fights are healed faster and cheaper in AQUES than anywhere else, so everyone has to visit.

## 16. How the characters look

Agreed 30 Oct.

- **What's wrong now:** the current creator bodies fail on every count: gym-model bodies, weak faces, bare underwear, a plastic smooth finish. They stay only until the real characters exist.
- **The look:** between grounded realism and stylised armour. Realistic bodies and faces; armour and cloth with a strong designed silhouette so a fighter reads at a glance from the over-the-shoulder camera.
- **Beginners wear commoner's rags:** plain medieval cloth, not underwear. Nation garb is bought or earned. (The opening film still shows the player's own clothes in our world.)
- **Faces:** 6 to 8 well-made preset faces per sex, no sliders.
- **Villains by type:** robbers in patched rags; deserters in stripped army gear; the Lord's soldiers and the Sentries in one uniform marked with the ear seal; nation bosses in their own armour.

## 17. Odds and ends

Agreed 30 Oct.

- **One rising per account, forever.** Nothing buys a re-roll. A new account starts from zero, carrying nothing over.
- **Vakna keep two tier ladders:** their Dim crafting tiers and new Lit fighting tiers, so they have more ways to grow strong.
- **Voices:** text with short spoken barks for now (a word, a grunt, a laugh, a Sentry's "hold!"). Full voice acting later.
- **Music:** a theme per nation and one for Chromara, each with a combat version that fights shift into.
- **The first ten minutes:** you land in your home nation in rags; a master finds you and gives you one fight to learn the controls, mentions the Allborn, and points you to town. No long tutorial.

## 8. The campaign: the search for the Allborn

Agreed 30 Oct.

### Plot
The player wants out, and everyone says only the Allborn can open the way. The campaign is the search, told in five parts. Each part is a lead the player chases, and each lead quietly removes one certainty.

| Part | Where | The lead | What it costs the player's certainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Coming of age | Home nation | The master hints the Allborn once trained here | The master's account doesn't match the one in the town market |
| 2. Going to town | Chromara | A merchant sells "proof": a relic said to be the Allborn's | The relic is a forgery, made by a Dim crafter for the Lord's treasury |
| 3. The four rumours | The other three nations | Each nation's master holds one rumour, guarded by that nation's level boss | The four rumours describe four different people |
| 4. The vanished Lord | Chromara and the border forts | The benign Lord vanished; the rumours tie it to the Allborn | The current Lord spreads the Allborn story on purpose, to keep the nations searching instead of rising |
| 5. The sealed statue | Chromara | The statue everyone believes holds the Allborn | A mystery, even to us for now |

### Mode
- Parts 1 and 2 are the beginning story mode: guided, one lead at a time, with the first financial decisions.
- Parts 3 and 4 are open: the three nations can be taken in any order, alone or with a guild.
- Part 5 is a finale event shared by the whole world.

### Story
The two threads (the Allborn and the Lord) start apart and meet in part 4, when the player learns the myth is the Lord's tool. The player is never told that the Allborn doesn't exist. The world only hints at it, softly: it's the hope that kills. Some players will never work it out.

Still open, on purpose: what is inside the statue, and whether there is a way out of the world at all. Nobody decides these yet.

### Battle
Each part ends in a showdown: a street gang boss (1), the forger's guards in Chromara's back streets (2), each nation's level boss (3), a border fort held by the Lord's soldiers (4), and the world boss (5).

Between those, showdowns come as often as they can without becoming a chore: the player should rarely go long without a fight, but never feel blocked by one.

## 9. The world boss

Agreed 30 Oct.

### Plot
The thing the old names were banished to contain. When the Lord unnamed the elements, whatever the names were holding loosened. It rises near Chromara and shifts element every few days.

### Mode
One shared health bar for the whole world; every player's fight chips at it. Rewards come from the treasury by damage done, with a large bonus for the final blow, which guilds race for. It returns stronger after it falls.

### Story
The Lord's soldiers fight it in public and blame the nations for it in private. Each nation's counter-element becomes valuable when it shifts, so prices move with it.

### Battle
A big arena fight that changes with each element phase: fire phases spread burning ground, water phases flood the arena and favour AQUES, earth phases raise stone, wind phases push players off ledges.


### How it plays (agreed 30 Oct)
- **It rises at set times** as a world event, for a few hours every few days, so everyone turns up at once.
- **Groups of about 20** fight in each copy of the arena, all hitting the one shared health bar. A counter shows the rest of the world fighting.
- **Falling in the fight** knocks you out of the arena; you rejoin after a short wait and lose nothing.
- **The Dim fight it too,** with weapons, relics, hands and legs; they just can't bend.
- **While it sleeps,** its next element leaks into the world: monsters of that element grow stronger and that shard's price climbs.
- **It has no name.** The Lord banned that too.

## 10. Money in the story

Agreed 30 Oct.

### Plot
CHROMA is the Lord's coin, stamped with an ear: the Lord is always listening. Fines for speaking the banished names go to the treasury, and so does a share of every trade in Chromara.

### Story
- The Dim are lowborn but hold the economy: only they craft from raw shards, and every Lit fighter depends on them for gear and repairs.
- A player's first financial decisions in the home nation (what to buy, what to sell, what to owe) set how they arrive in town.
- Getting rich makes you visible: the Sentries notice wealthy players, and trouble in Chromara can cost everything (section 4).

### Battle
Villains carry CHROMA, and whoever wins a fight takes the loser's loot. Robbers target players who carry too much.

### How the money works (agreed 30 Oct)
- **CHROMA is a real token on NEAR that costs nothing.** It is earned only by playing and is spent only in the game. There is no buying it with money and no cashing it out.
- **Everything is bought with CHROMA,** at home and away.
- **Counting houses.** You hand coin to a counting house and get a sealed note; any counting house honours it, so savings can be drawn anywhere. Chromara has 3 counting houses (more can be added later) and one real bank: a proper building with a full set-up. Each nation has 2 counting houses. Carrying coin to one is a journey, and robbers know the roads.
- **Marked players can't draw.** Counting houses answer to the Edict and refuse anyone with a red flag or a bounty.
- **Red flag:** a minor offence (speaking a banished name, brawling in town). It shows over your head, fades with time, and Sentries fine you on sight.
- **Bounty:** a serious offence. Your face goes on the notice boards with a price in CHROMA, and anyone who beats you collects it. It never fades; you pay it off in CHROMA or someone collects it.
- **Robbing** another player is allowed anywhere. Robbing and other evil deeds earn a red flag (Jadon, 1 Nov).
- **Three red flags** held at once, before they fade, become a bounty. That is the only road to a bounty.
- **Jail** seizes 40% of your CHROMA, materials and gear, vault included (land is never seized), and getting out costs a fine on top.
- **Trade between players** happens hand to hand, at stalls on owned land, and on a market board in Chromara. Most trade runs through the Dim. Trades between two Lit are taxed far more heavily than trades with the Dim: the Lit are hunters and looters, the Dim are the traders.
- **A merchant who goes broke** shuts their shop. A player can buy it; if nobody does, a new NPC merchant arrives after a while.
- **The treasury never runs dry.** The economy always favours the Lord. **Parked:** who sets taxes and fines.
